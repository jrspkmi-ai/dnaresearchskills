# Research method

1. Define the case, corpus boundary, sampling, arena and observation period.
2. Build a chronology using dates distinguishable from publication and access dates.
3. Map anchor sources and assess provenance and authenticity.
4. Discover related sources through documented searches and snowballing.
5. Preserve raw evidence separately; track duplicates, missingness and exclusions.
6. Code propositions: original speaker, concept, qualifier, document, evidence location and time. A quotation source is not automatically the speaker. Qualifiers are codebook-dependent, not general sentiment.
7. Define and justify event-based episodes; moving windows are an alternative analytical design.
8. Review coding against source evidence, create or update the DNA database, and execute DNA/rDNA. Keep an audit trail; AI suggestions require a human decision.
9. Interpret each network in relation to its construction rules, corpus and evidence.

Affiliation joins actor and concept; one-mode projections summarize relations through shared concepts and qualifiers. Edges do not establish direct interaction. Copied quotations may be dissemination traces without independent statements. Deduplication decisions depend on the question. DNA alone does not establish reach, virality or causation. Distinguish missing evidence from evidence of absence. Preserve uncertainty, extraction context, negative cases and coverage limits.
